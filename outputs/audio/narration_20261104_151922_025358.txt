You step outside and look up. A second sun has just appeared in the sky.

No warning. No sound. Just a blinding white dot, growing larger every second.

Ten seconds pass, and the air around you begins to burn.

Sixty-six million years ago, this was the final moment for the dinosaurs. So what happens if a ten-kilometer rock hits Earth today?

Right now, that asteroid is tearing through the atmosphere at seventy thousand kilometers per hour. Upon impact, it releases the energy of one billion nuclear bombs in a single millisecond.

Here is the terrifying part. It is not the blast that kills most of the planet. It is the dust.

Billions of tons of pulverized rock shoot into the stratosphere, wrapping the Earth in a pitch-black shroud. Sunlight vanishes. Global temperatures plunge into a deep freeze. Without light, photosynthesis stops, plants die, and the entire food chain collapses in months.

Now look up at the night sky. You are alive today only because that ancient rock missed our ancestors, and gave us the time to look back up and watch for the next one.
