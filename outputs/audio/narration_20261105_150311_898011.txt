You cut your hand on a sharp stone. It barely bleeds. Before antibiotics, even a cut that small could lead to a deadly infection.

Ancient healers dressed wounds with honey, used ant jaws as stitches, and drilled teeth with stone tools. 

Some even drilled into skulls. New bone around those holes shows that some patients survived.

At Shanidar Cave, a Neanderthal lived for years after serious injuries. 

Someone may have helped him eat and get around.

Now imagine that cut again. This time, someone kneels beside you and takes your hand. 

Maybe the oldest medicine began when someone saw you were hurt and chose to stay.
